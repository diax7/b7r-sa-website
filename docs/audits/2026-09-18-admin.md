## Summary

The shell is well built and consistent with its own design system: five groups, one hue each,
an icon on every entity, a description and a "Shows on" line on every list and form, a
description on 330 of the 366 fields an editor sees (90%; the rest are the runs' log columns, the redirect plugin's fields and a handful of read-only numbers), a palette, a preview button. What Dhia feels
comes from four structural things, not from polish:

1. **The sidebar has two different open/close mechanisms by screen width.** Above 1440 px it is
   our sidebar with a collapse button at its foot and an icon rail; at 1440 px and under (every
   MacBook Air and most 14" laptops at their default scaling) Payload's drawer takes over: the
   sidebar starts closed, a hamburger sits in the header and a chevron inside the drawer closes
   it. The group chevrons rotate but the rows look like entries; the counts on the right read as
   badges; the rail is 26 icons with hairlines and no labels. Section 1 has the proposal.
2. **Bilingual editing is one language per page load.** 131 fields are localized (103 text,
   21 textarea, 2 rich text, 2 arrays, 2 uploads, 1 number). Writing a product in both languages
   field by field costs 18 locale switches; the home page 118; each switch is a navigation that
   reloads the form. On phones the "Editing the Arabic content" note overlaps the status bar and
   the tabs (blocker). Section 4 has the facts and the design constraints.
3. **The text is mostly good, but three vocabularies collide.** "Slug", "Hero", "Eyebrow",
   "Lead", "CTA", "Meta title", "Ribbon", "Chips" are jargon on the English side; on the Arabic
   side «القسم» names three different things (a blog hub, a FAQ group, a page section) and
   «الحد الشهري» two (posts, dollars). Every enum select (topic status, run kind and status,
   FAQ group, platform, redirect type) shows raw code values. The panel's own strings (259 in
   `strings.ts`) and the visibility rules (about 75 sentences) exist in English only, so the
   Arabic admin is a translation job before it is an RTL job. Section 2 has the table.
4. **The dashboard tells an owner what the server is, not how the business is doing.** Ten
   "System status" rows (seven amber on the review server: environment facts nobody can act on
   from the admin), eight latest saves, and three good cards (score, traffic, engine). Missing:
   visits over 7 and 30 days with the trend, the cited rate, drafts waiting, what published
   this week, the spend against the limits, the next scheduled runs. Section 5 lists every
   number the database can already answer and where it comes from.

Two defects found on the way, both real: the JSON fields (runs' rubric, steps and outline,
the snapshots' data, the citations' links) render empty because Payload's JSON editor loads
Monaco from `cdn.jsdelivr.net`, which the admin CSP blocks (major); the palette lists media
hits above product hits for a product-name query because document hits are not ranked
(minor). axe found no serious or critical issue on our own surfaces; the five it found are
Payload's chrome (list checkboxes without labels, three icon-only popup buttons, the tab list).

Counts: 19 collections, 5 globals, 2 custom views; 26 sidebar entries for an admin, 11 for an
editor; 423 fields, 231 required, 131 localized; 259 English-only shell strings; 32 rule guides
and about 30 rule titles in English only.

Grades: **blocker** 2 · **major** 14 · **minor** 26 (the list per section; the fix order at

### Cloudflare's pattern, and the two shapes it can take

What Cloudflare's dashboard does that Dhia likes, in the parts that transfer to a CMS:

- One sidebar, about 256 px, on a surface a step lighter than the page, with a hairline to the
  content. The account switcher at the top; the search, notifications and the account menu in
  the page header, not in the sidebar.
- Groups are rows with a chevron at the end; the whole row toggles; children indent under a
  thin guide line; the group row stays visibly a header (heavier, no disc, more space above it
  than below).
- The active child has a tinted background and a coloured bar on the leading edge; the active
  group stays open.
- The collapse control is one button at the bottom of the sidebar ("Collapse sidebar", the
  double chevron). Collapsed, the sidebar is an icon rail of the **groups**, and a group's
  children open as a flyout panel on click or hover; the rail is the same element, not a second
  layout.
- On narrow screens the same sidebar is a drawer under the same hamburger; nothing else changes.

Shape A, **the two-tier sidebar** (a slim primary rail of groups, a wide secondary panel with
the selected group's entries): five group icons in a 64 px rail on the leading edge, the
selected group's entries in a 224 px panel beside it. Strong when there are ten groups with
thirty entries each. For five groups with 2 to 9 entries it costs a click to see any entry
outside the current group, hides the whole map, and doubles the RTL and drawer work (two
panels to mirror and to stack on a phone).

Shape B, **one sidebar with collapsible groups and the collapse toggle at the bottom**: the
whole map visible, groups fold, the toggle where the hand rests, the rail derived from the
same tree. This is Cloudflare's actual dashboard shape and it fits five groups with 26
entries.

**Recommendation: shape B**, with the rail showing groups only and a flyout for a group's
entries.

### Proposal

Structure (the registry `ADMIN_NAV` stays; only the rendering and three placements change):

```
[Brand]  B7R Print Website                       (links to the dashboard)
 ⌂ Dashboard                                     (a real entry, active on /admin)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ● Site                                       ⌄  (group row: 40 px, weight 600, no disc, chevron at the end)
    │ Home page
    │ Pages
    │ Media
    │ Site settings                               (admins)
 ● Catalogue                                  ›
 ● Blog                                       ⌄
    │ Posts
    │   Hubs · Authors · Tags                     (secondary, 12 px indent under Posts, guide line)
    │ Content engine                              (a sub-header inside Blog, admins)
    │   Topics · Runs · Engine settings
 ● Visibility                                 ›
 ● Admin                                      ›
──────────────────────────────────────────────────
 « Collapse sidebar                              (one button, full row, the same place at every width)
 [D] Dhia · admin@b7r.sa                      ⌄  (account menu: My account, Language, Log out)
```

Sizes and states:

- Width 264 px open; rail 64 px; drawer 100% under 768 px, 320 px between 768 and 1024.
- Group row: 40 px, 8 px above each group, a 1 px hairline between groups (not between
  entries), the label at 14 px weight 600 in the text colour (no uppercase, no disc: the disc is
  the entry's mark, the group's mark is its colour dot 8 px before the label).
- Entry: 36 px, 14 px, a 24 px disc in the group's hue; secondary entry 32 px, 13 px, no disc,
  a 2 px guide line on the leading edge of the indented block.
- Active entry: the group's tint plus a 3 px bar on the leading edge in the group's hue, weight
  500; the parent group forced open. Hover: `surface-2`. Focus: the accent ring.
- Counts: gone from the sidebar (they belong on the list page's header and the dashboard).
  Keep a badge only for a number that asks for action: failed jobs on Runs, drafts waiting on
  Posts, an over-limit connection on Connections. Red or amber, never the muted grey.
- Collapse: one button, the last row above the account, `«` when open and `»` when closed (the
  glyph mirrors in RTL). The state persists in the `nav` preference as today. The rail keeps
  the five group icons, the dashboard icon and the account avatar; a click on a group icon
  opens a 224 px flyout with the group's entries (a Radix `Popover`, focus moved in, Esc closes);
  the active group's icon carries the bar.
- Breakpoints: one rule. Above 1024 px (Payload's `m`) the sidebar is inline, open or rail,
  our button; at 1024 px and under it is a drawer opened by the hamburger and closed by the
  same hamburger (an X), Esc, or a tap outside; the collapse button hides in the drawer. The
  1440 px special case goes: override Payload's `l` behaviour by keeping the `template-default`
  grid column and the nav visible from 1025 px up (the CSS that does it for 1441 today).
- Keyboard: Tab through group rows and entries in order; Enter or Space toggles a group; Arrow
  Up and Down move between rows inside the sidebar (roving `tabindex`); Home and End; typing a
  letter jumps to the next entry starting with it (a `nav` with `aria-label`, groups as
  `button[aria-expanded]` owning a `ul` with `role="group"` and `aria-labelledby`; `aria-current`
  on the active link). Ctrl K stays the palette; `g` then `s` / `c` / `b` / `v` / `a` could
  jump to a group later, not now.
- Narrow screens: the drawer keeps the full tree with the same rows at 44 px (touch), the
  account and the language switch at the foot, the hamburger in the header at the leading edge,
  the palette and "View website" as icons at the trailing edge. No second breakpoint.
- Header: the search box shrinks to 240 px and grows on focus; "View website" keeps its text;
  Payload's account avatar is hidden (`.app-header__account { display: none }` in
  `@layer payload`) since our account block owns it; the locale switcher stays until the
  side-by-side editing lands, then leaves for the document header (section 4).
- Dashboard controls Dhia mentioned: the page header of a view carries its own controls at the
  trailing edge (7 / 30 / 90 days, "Pull now", "Recompute") as today; the dashboard gets a
  range control the same way (section 5).

### How the switch works

Payload's `localization` has two locales (`ar` default, `en`, `fallback: true`;
`cms/index.ts:77-84`). The edit form loads **one locale per request**: the locale is a query
parameter (`?locale=en`) and a stored preference (`locale`, in `payload-preferences`), the
header's "Locale: العربية" popup changes it, and the change is a full navigation of the form.
Every localized label carries the `AR` / `EN` pill (`admin.css:297-328`) and a note line sits
before the document controls (`locale-note.tsx`). The document menu offers Payload's "Copy to
locale" (copies every localized value of the document from one locale to the other),
"Publish in العربية" (publishes the open locale only) and "Duplicate Selected Locales". Reads
outside the form can ask for `?locale=all` and get `{ ar, en }` per localized field; the form
never does. Autosave (1.5 s, pages, posts, products, testimonials, the home page) saves the
open locale only; globals without drafts (site settings, search defaults, engine settings)
must be saved before a switch (Payload asks before leaving).

### Payload's constraints for a side-by-side design

- The form owns one locale; `useField` reads and writes that locale's value; a custom field
  component (`admin.components.Field`) can render anything but its `setValue` writes the open
  locale only.
- A second locale's value can be **read** in the component (`GET
  /api/payload/<collection>/<id>?locale=en&depth=0`, or `?locale=all` once and shared by every
  field through a context) and **written** only by a second request
  (`PATCH …?locale=en&draft=true` with the changed fields), never through the form's own submit.
  A hook cannot do it either: `beforeChange` sees the open locale's data.
- Localized arrays (post takeaways) and per-language uploads (hero photos) are per-locale as a
  whole; a leaf-level twin does not apply to them.
- Rich text is a Lexical editor per locale; two editors on one page are possible (Payload renders
  one per field) but heavy on a post with a long body; a "show English beside" toggle per rich
  text field rather than always-on.
- Validation runs on the open locale; the second request validates the other on its own and can
  refuse (a required English title still empty): the twin must show that refusal beside the
  field.
- Autosave fires on the open locale's dirty state; the twin's edits need their own debounce
  and a "saved" tick, or a "Save both" that chains the form submit and the second request.
- `fallback: true` means a REST read of `en` returns the Arabic when English is empty; a twin
  must read with `?fallback-locale=none` (`fallbackLocale: false` in the Local API) or it will
  show Arabic as if it were the English.
- The list view shows the open locale's title; a document without an English title reads as
  "No Title" in the English list.

Approach to settle with the CTO (section 2 of the programme): a `Twin` field wrapper for
`text`, `textarea` and (later) `richText`, registered by `describeFields()` on every field with
`localized: true` (the same one pass that sets descriptions and cells), rendering the open
locale's control through `useField` and the other locale's control beside it (stacked under
768 px), reading the other locale once per document through a `TwinProvider` mounted from
`beforeDocumentControls` (`?locale=all&fallback-locale=none&draft=true`), writing it with one
`PATCH …?locale=<other>&draft=true` chained after the form's save (Payload's `useDocumentEvents`
`mostRecentUpdate` or `useForm().submit` wrapper) and on a 2 s debounce for drafts. 124 leaf
fields (103 text + 21 textarea) take the wrapper as is; 2 rich text fields take a "beside"
toggle; the 2 arrays and 2 uploads keep the locale switch. The locale switcher then leaves
the header for a "Preview as" control in the document sidebar, and the note line goes.
