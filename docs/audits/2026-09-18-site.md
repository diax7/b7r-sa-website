# Site audit (2026-09-18)

Phase 0 of `docs/plans/2026-09-18-prelaunch-programme.md`. Read-only. The production build
served on `http://localhost:3004` with the production origin (`https://b7r.sa` in every
canonical, hreflang, sitemap and JSON-LD URL); the review database (media `local`, contact
and newsletter `mock`, Turnstile `off`, Umami on the test stand-in).

Method: a Playwright crawl of all 54 public routes (27 Arabic, 27 English) plus the two 404s
at 390 px (head metadata, JSON-LD parsed, every `<img>`, console, every response); Lighthouse
12.6 on every route, mobile (simulated slow 4G, 4x CPU) and desktop, 108 runs after a cache
warm-up; screenshots of 8 routes per language at 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1920 px;
scripted interaction (contact and newsletter forms, language switch, designer, FAQ, hero,
keyboard, sticky bars, WhatsApp widget, 404 and 410); a curl pass on every internal link,
redirect and machine file. Numbers below are from those runs; Windows headless Chrome makes
Lighthouse's observed first paint noisy, the simulated scores were stable across runs (±2).

## After the fixes (2026-09-18)

Item 12 of the fix order, on top of PR #38 (items 1 to 6, 10, 13 to 18). Same method as
the table above for the "after" columns: the production build with the production origin,
the image transforms warmed, Lighthouse 12.6 mobile (simulated slow 4G, 4x CPU), one run per
route on the same Windows machine (a second for the routes the shared machine skewed, see the
paragraph under the table), this time with GA and Umami ids on the database (the consent
card mounts, as in CI). Mobile only; "Perf before" is the table above.

**The analyzer.** `@next/bundle-analyzer` is a webpack plugin and the build is Turbopack
(Next 16's default), so the pass used Next's own `next experimental-analyze --output` (the
same data, per route, per chunk, per module, no dependency to add) plus the served HTML's
script list per route (what a browser fetches before hydration: the `noModule` polyfill is
excluded, browsers never fetch it). Before, in KB gzip: the home and the product pages 182,
the blog 179, every CMS page 206 (`/contact` included: it shipped the same 206 as `/privacy`).
Of the 182 on the home, 140 is the framework (React DOM 73, the app router 43, React and the
navigation hooks 9, the layout router 4, the error boundaries 3, the Turbopack runtime 4, the
root error boundary 4) and 42 is ours. What the analyzer found in ours, largest first:

1. **The contact form on every CMS page (30 KB gzip in four chunks).** Every designed page
   imports the block registry (`src/app/(site)/cms-blocks.ts`), the registry's contact block
   imported `ContactForm` statically, and Turbopack emits every client module reachable from
   a route's server graph in that route's entry scripts, rendered or not. So `/privacy`,
   `/terms`, `/shipping`, `/how-it-works`, `/about`, `/faq` and the compare page carried Radix
   Select (6 KB), floating-ui (10 KB), the dismissable layer, focus scope and remove-scroll
   (8 KB), the Turnstile hook, the popper and the form itself, and evaluated them on a 4x
   slowed CPU before anything could be tapped. This is the table's "CMS pages at 243 KB against
   205", and the answer to both questions the CTO note asked. The "67 KB more on `/contact`":
   the contact page shipped the same 206 KB first paint as the other CMS pages; the 28 KB
   Lighthouse recorded on top was Cloudflare's Turnstile `api.js`, which the form mounts at
   load when a site key is configured (it is, on the audit's machine and this one), so the
   whole 67 is the form and Turnstile, as expected. The compare page's 250 ms TBT: its table
   is a server component and hydrates nothing of its own; the TBT was the form group's
   evaluation (about 100 KB raw of Radix and floating-ui) on every CMS page, and one run's
   noise on top (the Arabic compare page read 170 ms on the same JS). The fix is a client
   loader with the `import()` (`contact-form-loader.tsx`): a `next/dynamic` inside the server
   component was tried first and changed nothing, the split has to sit in a client module.
   The form stays server-rendered and hydrates with the page on `/contact`, where its chunks
   are preloaded; nowhere else.
2. **The FAQ accordion on all 54 routes (6 KB gzip).** PR #38 rendered the Radix accordion
   directly from the server components (right for the CLS) and re-exported it from the
   `@/modules/core` barrel, which the site document imports for the header and footer. That
   put `@radix-ui/react-accordion`, collection, collapsible and their helpers in the root
   layout's chunk group, on the product pages and the blog too. This is the 4 KB that pushed
   the home (187,766 bytes) and the blog index (185,597) over the 180 KB budget on PR #38's
   CI run. Now the server renders `FaqClosedList`, the same boxes and classes as the Radix
   accordion closed (from one set of constants in `accordion-styles.ts`; the buttons
   `aria-disabled`, focusable, so nothing looks live before it is and a Tab onto one mounts
   the island and keeps the focus, the CTO's m1), and `FaqAccordionLoader` mounts the island
   near the viewport, or the moment a Tab or a finger reaches a row, over it. The heights with and without JavaScript still match (the e2e from PR #38
   holds); the answers stay in the DOM for crawlers and in `FAQPage`.
3. **The barrel in client components (2 to 4 KB gzip per route).** Turbopack keeps a
   re-exported module's imports in the client graph even when its exports go unused, so a
   client import of `@/modules/core` (the error boundaries, the designer, the newsletter, the
   product tracker) pulled the footer's four brand glyphs and the status page into the hero
   chunk and the error boundary's chunk of every route. Client components now import the
   module they need; the barrel says it is for server components.
4. **The root error boundary (3.3 KB gzip).** `global-error.tsx` is its own bundle, and its
   `next/link` cost every page a second copy of the router's link runtime. Plain anchors: when
   the root has failed, a full load is the way back.
5. **The WhatsApp glyph (1.7 KB gzip).** The four brand paths shared one module and Turbopack
   keeps a module whole, so the widget and the error boundary carried X, Instagram and TikTok
   too. The WhatsApp glyph has its own module.
6. **The newsletter form (1.5 KB gzip).** A client component in every footer; the markup now
   lives once in `NewsletterFields`, rendered inert by the server (the field read-only, the
   button `aria-disabled`, both focusable) and taken over by the island near the viewport or
   on the first Tab or tap (`NewsletterLoader`).
7. **A stand-in that vanished while its chunk loaded.** `NearViewport` mounted `next/dynamic`
   islands with no `loading`, so the server-rendered stand-in disappeared for the length of
   the download and the box collapsed (the static designer while Konva's 119 KB arrived, the
   FAQ rows while Radix did): the first hydrated run of the FAQ page measured every group at
   52 px. The host now renders the stand-in itself, outside the island's Suspense boundary,
   and drops it in the island's layout effect, so exactly one stand-in is in the DOM at any
   moment (a Suspense boundary showing the same node as its fallback duplicated it on a
   dehydrated boundary in CI); the designer's stand-in matches the island's price rows, and a
   deep link jumps instantly, as a fragment landing does, then once more after the mount.

Left as they are, with the numbers: the consent bar and the WhatsApp widget were already
behind `AfterDelay` and `dynamic()` (1.1 and 2.5 KB gzip, at 0.8 s and 1.5 s; the consent
bar's `use-bottom-dock` rides in a 5 KB chunk Turbopack shares with the designer's price
helpers, after the paint); the analytics bridge stays immediate (0.9 KB: it owns the delegated
click tracking the e2e proves and a deferred bridge would lose a click in its first second);
Radix Tooltip is on no site page; the Turnstile hook lives in the form's chunk and loads with
it on `/contact` only. The two largest pieces of ours that remain in every first paint are
`tailwind-merge` (8.5 KB gzip, `cn()` on every component) and `next/image`'s client runtime
(6 KB, the header logo and every photo); both are design-system decisions, not islands, and
are the next lever if one is ever needed.

**After, in KB gzip before hydration:** the home and the product pages 168 (from 182), the
blog 168 (from 179), every CMS page 164 (from 206), `/contact` 199 (from 206; its own form,
35 KB in five preloaded chunks, is the difference). The budget e2e's measure (every script
within 1.5 s of `load`, prefetches aborted, the consent card counted): the home 172,265 bytes
on Chromium desktop, 173,415 on the Pixel 7 profile, 171,980 on the iPhone 15 profile (168 KB,
12 KB under the 184,320 budget); the blog index 173,448 (169 KB). Budgets untouched.
